System Overview

FlexiCup is a wireless multimodal suction cup featuring dual-zone vision-tactile sensing. The central zone dynamically switches between vision and tactile modalities through LED illumination control, enabling precise contact detection. The peripheral zone maintains continuous spatial awareness for approach planning. Supporting both vacuum and Bernoulli suction modes through modular configurations, FlexiCup achieves complete wireless autonomy with onboard computation and power. Key specifications include 41.5N maximum normal force, 60,248 pixels·cm⁻² tactile resolution, and 1024×768@30Hz video streaming over Wi-Fi.

Hardware Design

System Integration and Architecture

The system integrates modular components including pneumatic airways, optical system (OV5640 camera with 180° fisheye lens), electronics (ESP32S3 controller), and mechanical assembly. A wireless charging configuration enables standalone operation with a 3.7V 300mAh lithium battery, supporting both tethered and untethered modes for flexible deployment.

Vision-Tactile Sensing System

A single 180° fisheye camera enables dynamic switching between environmental vision and tactile perception through LED illumination control. The dual-zone architecture provides switchable visuotactile sensing in the central zone and continuous spatial awareness in the peripheral zone. This multimodal fusion achieves 100% object recognition accuracy, significantly outperforming vision-only (82.5%) and tactile-only (46.7%) approaches.

Dual-Zone Sensing

The peripheral zone maintains environmental awareness through continuous vision, while the central zone provides high-resolution tactile feedback via LED-controlled illumination switching. This architecture enables simultaneous spatial context understanding and precise contact detection for robust manipulation.

PDMS Membrane Design

The membrane features a dual-layer design: a PDMS base layer (30:1 ratio, 70°C×4h) provides mechanical compliance, while a semitransparent reflective layer (Ag:PDMS 100:1, 70°C×0.5h) enables photometric tactile imaging. Four modular configurations (I-IV) support vacuum (I-II) and Bernoulli (III-IV) operations, achieving adhesion forces from sub-Newton to over 30N.

System Characterization

Sensor-Based Force Characterization

To rigorously validate suction force capabilities, we conducted comprehensive measurements using a calibrated 6-axis force/torque sensor. Automated normal pull-off and tangential drag tests were performed on a flat acrylic surface at -80 kPa vacuum pressure, with 20 trials per test ensuring statistical reliability.

Force measurements at -80 kPa: normal force (pull-off) and tangential force (drag) averaged over 20 trials. Inset: experimental setup with 6-axis force/torque sensor.

Results: Measurements yielded a mean maximum normal force of 41.50 N and tangential (shear) force of 8.34 N. Compared to the theoretical baseline F = P × A using nominal diameter 23 mm (≈33.2 N), the measured normal force is higher, likely due to suction cup compliance increasing the effective sealing area under vacuum. Force profiles capture transient behaviors during attachment and detachment, providing comprehensive characterization of dynamic force characteristics, repeatability, and surface dependency.

Non-Contact Verification: Wafer Handling

To directly verify non-contact handling capability, we conducted wafer pick-up experiments comparing vacuum and Bernoulli suction modes. Results demonstrate that Bernoulli suction maintains near-zero contact force during lifting, while vacuum suction requires physical contact to establish a seal.

Dynamic Performance Evaluation

To evaluate real-time performance and dynamic robustness, we conducted stress tests at realistic motion speeds. After establishing suction, the robot performs fast motions (joint speed = 3.0 rad/s, acceleration = 3.0 rad/s²) while holding objects. Demonstrations include an orange and a water-filled bottle, where water sloshing causes significant mass distribution changes, yet the suction grasp remains stable with no sensing degradation.

Applications

Modular Perception-Driven Grasping

A modular control framework demonstrates threshold-based perception-driven grasping with dual-mode suction mechanisms. Experimental evaluation on LEGO board manipulation shows vacuum mode achieving 90.0% success rate and Bernoulli mode achieving 86.7% success rate. The system successfully handles object localization, obstacle detection, surface alignment, and contact confirmation through visuotactile sensing.

End-to-End Contact-Aware Manipulation

A diffusion policy network processes multiscale observations from workspace and dual-zone suction cameras through parallel ResNet-18 encoders. A multihead attention mechanism enables interaction between peripheral and central features, integrating visuotactile information for contact-aware manipulation. The unified framework outputs robot pose, illumination control, and valve state commands.

Inclined Transport Task

This task demonstrates six-stage manipulation: workspace observation for initial localization, suction camera-guided search, approach and alignment using peripheral vision, inclined transport with tactile contact detection, contact confirmation through central zone sensing, and final object lifting. The system achieves 73.3% success rate, with multihead attention improving performance by 13% over baseline approaches.

Orange Extraction Task

This task involves lid removal, re-alignment, and tactile-guided grasping of deformable objects from containers. The system achieves 66.7% success rate, demonstrating effective integration of multimodal sensing with learned control for contact-aware manipulation of compliant objects. Ablation studies confirm central zone sensing is essential for contact detection, while attention mechanisms significantly enhance feature coordination.
