System Overview

FlexiCup is a wireless multimodal suction cup that integrates dual-zone vision-tactile sensing. The central zone dynamically switches between vision and tactile modalities via illumination control for contact detection, while the peripheral zone provides continuous spatial awareness for approach planning. FlexiCup supports both vacuum and Bernoulli suction modes through modular mechanical configurations, achieving complete wireless autonomy with onboard computation and power. The system achieves maximum suction force of 34.3N with tactile resolution of 60,248 pixels·cm⁻², streaming 1024×768 images at 30Hz over Wi-Fi.

Hardware Design

System Integration and Architecture

The system features modular component integration including airways, optical system (OV5640 camera with 180° fisheye lens), electronics (ESP32S3 controller), and mechanical assembly. Wireless charging configuration enables standalone operation with a 3.7V 300mAh lithium battery, supporting both tethered and wireless modes.

Vision-Tactile Sensing System

A single 180° fisheye camera enables dynamic switching between environmental vision and precise tactile perception through LED illumination control. The dual-zone sensing system provides central zone switchable visuotactile sensing and peripheral zone continuous spatial awareness. Multimodal fusion achieves 100% recognition accuracy across diverse object categories, significantly outperforming vision-only (82.5%) and tactile-only (46.7%) approaches.

Dual-Zone Sensing

The peripheral zone maintains environmental awareness through continuous vision across both modalities, while the central zone provides high-resolution tactile feedback through LED-controlled illumination switching. This architecture enables simultaneous spatial context understanding and precise contact detection.

PDMS Membrane Design

The membrane consists of a dual-layer system with PDMS base layer (30:1 ratio, 70°C×4h) providing mechanical compliance and semitransparent reflective layer (Ag:PDMS 100:1, 70°C×0.5h) enabling photometric tactile imaging. Four modular configurations (I-IV) support both vacuum (I-II) and Bernoulli (III-IV) operations, achieving adhesion force ranges from sub-Newton to over 30N.

System Characterization

Sensor-Based Force Characterization

To provide rigorous validation of suction force capabilities, we conducted comprehensive force measurements using a calibrated 6-axis force/torque sensor. Automated normal pull-off and tangential drag tests were performed on a flat acrylic surface at -80 kPa vacuum pressure, with 20 trials for each test to ensure statistical reliability.

Force profiles over 20 trials: normal force (pull-off test) and tangential force (horizontal drag test) at -80 kPa. Inset shows experimental setup with FlexiCup and 6-axis force/torque sensor.

Experimental Results: The measurements yielded a mean maximum normal force of 41.50 N and a mean maximum tangential (shear) force of 8.34 N. Compared to the theoretical baseline F = P × A using the nominal resting diameter of 23 mm (≈33.2 N), the measured normal force is higher, likely due to the compliance of the suction cup increasing the effective sealing area under vacuum. The force profiles also capture transient behaviors during attachment and detachment, providing comprehensive characterization of dynamic force characteristics including repeatability and surface dependency.

Wafer Pick-up Comparison

To directly verify non-contact handling capability, we conducted a wafer pick-up experiment comparing vacuum and Bernoulli suction modes. The experiment demonstrates that Bernoulli suction maintains near-zero contact force during lifting, while vacuum suction requires physical contact to establish a seal.

Dynamic Performance at Realistic Speed

To evaluate real-time performance and dynamic robustness, we conducted stress tests at realistic motion speeds. After establishing suction, the robot performs fast motions (joint speed = 3.0 rad/s, joint acceleration = 3.0 rad/s²) while holding objects. The demonstrations include holding an orange and a water-filled bottle. The water sloshing in the bottle causes significant mass distribution and center-of-mass changes during motion, yet the suction grasp remains stable. The sensing stream also remains stable with no degradation of image quality.

Applications

Modular Perception-Driven Grasping

The modular control framework demonstrates threshold-based perception-driven grasping with dual-mode suction mechanisms. Experimental evaluation on LEGO board manipulation tasks shows vacuum mode achieving 90.0% success rate and Bernoulli mode achieving 86.7% success rate. The system successfully handles object localization, obstacle detection, surface alignment, and contact confirmation through visuotactile sensing.

End-to-End Contact-Aware Manipulation

The diffusion policy network processes multiscale observations from workspace and dual-zone suction cameras through parallel ResNet-18 encoders. A multihead attention mechanism enables interaction between peripheral and central features, integrating visuotactile information for contact-aware manipulation. The unified framework outputs robot pose, illumination control, and valve state commands.

Inclined Transport Task

The inclined transport task demonstrates six-stage manipulation: workspace observation for initial localization, suction camera-guided search, approach and alignment using peripheral vision, inclined transport with tactile contact detection, contact confirmation through central zone sensing, and final object lifting. The system achieves 73.3% success rate with multihead attention mechanism improving performance by 13% compared to baseline approaches.

Orange Extraction Task

The orange extraction task involves lid removal, re-alignment, and tactile-guided grasping of deformable objects from containers. The system achieves 66.7% success rate, demonstrating the effectiveness of integrating multimodal sensing with learned control for contact-aware manipulation of compliant objects. Ablation studies confirm that central zone sensing is essential for contact detection while attention mechanisms significantly enhance feature coordination.

Baseline Comparison: BC-RNN vs. Diffusion Policy

To evaluate the effectiveness of the diffusion policy approach, we implemented a BC-RNN (Behavioral Cloning with RNN) baseline using the same multimodal observations and training data. The BC-RNN baseline consistently failed to complete both tasks, typically getting stuck near the target objects without progressing to successful manipulation. This demonstrates the advantage of diffusion policies for complex contact-aware manipulation tasks.
